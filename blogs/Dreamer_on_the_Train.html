Dreamer on the Train

August 18, 2018

It was raining heavily back home.

Drip, Drip, Drop.

It’d been hours, and she was there, waiting at the station. The train was late and apparently, she was the only one who didn’t get the information on time. All alone, with the station-master and a few regular people as an exception, sitting in the lounge, with her iPad, her books, her stories, she starts to drift away.

The bench had become warm by now, but the air seemed to talk to her bones, chilling them to their core as if it carried with itself horror stories from another dimension. She took off her coat and pulled it in front of her, in a desperate attempt to close the listening aids of her bones, in a negligibly effective move to avoid the air from hitting her.

She looks above, the skies are dark as if it’s London in the old days, or a romantic movie perhaps, with the rain and all, and a couple in the middle of the path with the view grey as it is, becoming coloured only where they stood. Hopefully it wasn’t the rail-track where they expressed their affection for it might mean the end of their love story as she was expecting the train any time now.*Giggle*

“Excuse me miss. I’ve been want-‘”

“Wha-“, she was startled at the voice of another soul. She didn’t hear any footsteps, or maybe it was just the rain hid them as curtains do light.

“I was saying, would you be so kind as to tell me what you are doing at this time of the hour over here at the train station? A little girl like you shouldn’t be alone around these areas. Where are your parents?”

She sharpened her gaze and tried to look more clearly, to identify that the station master had come over. Probably his shift got over and was concerned about her.

“Oh! I was waiting for a train. My parents have gone back to where they belong, Mister-?”

“Joshua. I’d say, what has this world come up to, letting a young lady such as yourself all alone at night over a place such as this. What about those goons that roam around here?”

“Well, I can take care of myself quite well you see. There’s nothing to be worried about. Thank you for your concern though.”

“If you say so, I’ll be on my way. Take care!”, the station-master departed.

Little Asha sat there by herself, all through the night till the morning when the train arrived, wondering about how fake the world is, like the station-master, pretending to care while all of it is just a way to tell to themselves how good they are. ‘Puh! If he was so nice, then why didn’t he stay the night, giving me company?’, she thought.

The train did arrive, and quite late one would say, but there was one thing off when it departed, Asha was not on it.
