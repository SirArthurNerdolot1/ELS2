The Circular Butterfly

June 9, 2020

It was a typical January evening accompanied by an eerie winter breeze. A crestfallen man trudged along the snow, hunched in the soft moonlight, an aura of paranoia and alertness around him, a war veteran I supposed. He moved briskly but silently, like a predator lurking in the dark, scouring his victim with those two perfect yellow eyes. Dark chased light as the final rays of hope vanished into the night and the man seemed to disappear with it.

My day had been a weird one, albeit the fact they all have the same level of monotony around them but today was especially weird. I looked at the scene with interest particularly because the mystery around the character impulsively induced a feeling of déjà vu. I couldn’t place it then and it didn’t seem to matter because I had a perfectly good gaming system set up inside my safe haven, considering how I was made to sit through the screening of The Butterfly Effect. The only thing that had piqued my attention today had left, that ancient trophy wading through the trail.

The cold wind made me realise I had left my jacket on the couch, and on top of that there was no one at the door. This was frustrating. In all of my rage of being called without any reason and my dreams of playing football professionally, I threw the cola can high into the air. It matched the sun on its trajectory and I returned before it could hit the road.

********************************************************************************

I opened my eyes. I knew it was my job to pry out of the cryo chamber immediately, but the sleep had taken its toll on my physicality. My first impulse was to throw up. Alarms blared in the distance, lights flashing around, but everything was in a daze. I remember thinking about how beautiful life seemed but I had a job to do. I rushed to the bridge. My training had taught me that this situation meant death but how does one give up on hope. Experimental missions have their own chances at risk, mostly those that are off the books too. We were never supposed to return but in the chaos and flames, I could not figure out what went haywire. If I couldn’t have saved the ship then no one could have. I turned to Camera 12A to see the calm expressions on the faces of my crew. I frantically hit the buttons, and in the process thanked them for their service. Then everything went white, a symphony played in the background as I went to sleep again.

********************************************************************************

Blood flowed through the drains. The rain only added to the mess. Bullet shells on the field and a few policemen interspersed with bullets, catching their last breaths. Over at the place where the inmate escaped was the body of a prisoner stabbed in the throat. A walkie-talkie switched from static to signal, “Inmate AG567 escaped custody today. Be advised subject is extremely dangerous and highly delusional, considers himself to be a little girl. Send ambulances over to the scene. Four dead and 3 critical in the escape carried out. The subject and accomplices escaped in an ice cream van, I repeat, an ice cream van.”

********************************************************************************

The cola reached the peak of its trajectory and landed on the sidewalk, with a single person to witness the event who kept his pace on.

Unaware of his surroundings, well lost in his thoughts, the strange looking man with a strange sounding name tripped on the can. He hit the sidewalk hard, cursed his fate and got up in pain. He resolved to confront the perpetrator and followed him to his house. The person invited himself in to give him a piece of his mind. After about a minute of a heated argument, presumably over a can but more accurately over the ethics and procedure of can throwing, he noticed the resident’s gaming set-up. It promised the quintessential change in technology, major modifications never seen before, running a game no one had ever heard of before. He asked to be let in to study it in a greater detail. Closer inspection revealed almost fantastic research ideas, exquisite modified setups, proper codes and exceptional innovation. The resident was the perfect candidate for the CONSILIUM mission. The person, a professor, invited the resident to become a part of the space confidentiality, to lead a mission off the books, something so extravagant such that it could not be defined in normal terms. The professor knew that he was rushing things even after a couple years of training but it was essential to do so.

I took the lead of the CONSILIUM mission. I did not know the exact purpose. Rather no one knew, everyone knew only parts of the final solution, compartmentalisation we called it. It gave me the opportunity to get out of the house and maybe as cheesy as it sounds, to use my abilities to my potential. As we got into Earth’s orbit, all everyone had was second thoughts. We were never going to return again. The professor had chosen all of us as we had nothing to do and would rather be part of something rather than sit alone at home. It had worked. He was pretty successful in setting up a team and we had embarked on our mission. I still didn’t know the purpose behind the mission. We were promised all answers when we reached Europa, the place where we would finally understand the mission that was apparently supposed to be the most important mission till date, something so confidential that even the team involved could not be trusted with the information let alone the general public. Bubbling with curiosity we all entered cryo sleep.

Little did I know.

I opened my eyes again. This time in a stark white room. It was a mental asylum. 3 years had passed since the crash. I was not supposed to be alive but one cannot mess with causality. The doctors explained to me the events that had happened, how the mission failed, how the crew died. How PTSD and vertigo were to be my defining traits now. Therapy and Counselling outlined my day.

Those times were tough.

But they were happy to me.

I played in the garden, under the sun and shade, on top of trees and hiding throughout the space. I felt joy in that space.

I learnt a new word in those days.

“Schizophrenia”

Years passed.

And people thought that I was going to be completely okay.

Little did they know.

The next time I opened my eyes again was in a different garden. I was introduced to my parents. I liked them. I stayed there for years I think. But I craved some ice-cream. I guess they did too. Because they fell to the ground in joy when they were showered with the rainbow sprinkles. I felt proud. I got on the van and we left riding on a rainbow towards my dreams.

Finally Free.

I lost my garden that day. The man dropped me off at a house. I didn’t see a garden rather a house that felt my own. I looked back, wondering where I was these past few years. I turned back. The ice cream man was the professor, the same guy who had given me the job. How strange to have snapped out of it.

What was the word again?

“Schizophrenia”

Had it ended, I didn’t really care.

I asked the professor about the mission and how it had failed.

“It did work exactly how it was supposed to.” The professor said.

I didn’t understand. Maybe my confused expression demanded an explanation.

“I know you don’t understand. But realise, remember, comprehend, scrutinise this, Time is now circular.”

I looked into the mirror. I was an old man now. I looked exactly like the professor.

He asked me out on a movie. I agreed.

“The Butterfly Effect.”

What an utter piece of crap it was.

I returned back home, trudging along the snow. It was a typical January evening accompanied by an eerie winter breeze.

I noticed someone staring at me from across the road. A can of cola in his hand. I looked at the scene with interest particularly because the mystery around the character impulsively induced a feeling of déjà vu…
