After Thought

November 30, 2020

Time.

It’s always fascinated me.

Something that’s continuous, but also immovable. It’s one thing we can always rely on. Whether we eat or sleep, have fun or work hard, celebrate or grieve, all of it consumes time. You can do anything you want in this world, but every single action you take is governed by the laws of time.

And that time is limited. That’s the solitary reason for the significance of anything we do. If we had unlimited time, we could and eventually would do every activity in the universe. The fact that we can’t do it all, that we only have a limited number of moves left to make before we’re done, is what gives any of those moves purpose.

But I digress. I’m trying to distract myself from what is probably the most important choice I will ever make.

As I step inside the room, I’m blinded by the immediate brightness. From top to bottom, the room is completely and utterly white, aside from one wooden desk in the exact center, with two wooden chairs on either side. On the chair farther from me sits a Latin woman, maybe in her mid-thirties, wearing a white lab coat over a white shirt. I see her name, Sara, scribbled hastily on the lab coat with a marker. I carefully make my way over to the empty chair and sit down. Two buttons sit on the desk in front of me. A simple green ‘YES’ and a red ‘NO’. Large, physical buttons, as if having an actual physical presence lends more importance to the choice.

She says to me, “It’s time for you to make a final decision now.”

I know that I should focus on what I’m going to decide, but I just can’t help wondering how it’s come to this for me. I should be at home right now, sipping a glass of wine on my pristine leather couch while watching some junk on TV.

But if I’m really being honest, I never really did anything like that, even in the good days. Wall Street never sleeps, and nor do it’s brokers, so my life was constantly clouded by never-ending stress. When it all fell apart for me, as it often does for many like me, I couldn’t help but feel just a little bit of relief. Perhaps I wouldn’t have felt that relief had I known what the next three years had in store for me.

The thing is, there’s really nowhere for a broker to go after Wall Street. Once you’ve been kicked out, you can’t force your way back. And no one would prefer a washed up 34 year old guy as their consultant to a shiny new 22 year old, just out of college and with just enough brash confidence to win everyone over. Odd jobs, managing at coffee shops, pharmaceutical trials, I’ve tried everything I could to make money. And while it’s enough for me to survive, it’s just not enough for me to live.

Well, everything could change now. It all started this morning, when two men came to my single-room flat. I was surprised to hear the doorbell ring in the first place, because it’s been a long time since I’ve had any visitors. They were wearing identical three-piece suits, and they looked just official enough to let me know that they weren’t here for a mere meet and greet.

“Sir, we’d like to talk to you for a few minutes. Can we sit down?”

As they got comfortable on my lone couch, well, at least as comfortable as you can get on the dusty old couch that came with the flat, I noisily dragged my plastic chair in front of the couch so as to face them.

They got straight to the point.

“Sir, me and my colleague here are part of a governmental agency, we’d prefer not to say which, and we have a request to make of you.”

I let out a small sigh of relief under my breath. I was almost convinced that these men had come to apprehend me for some of the less than ethical things I did in my Wall Street days.

The other man spoke, “We’re conducting a trial, a research experiment of sorts, and we’ve been looking for potential candidates. And we’d like to inform you that you, sir, are one of those candidates.”

I was confused.

“What, uh, what kind of trial?”

“Something our R&D department came up with, a drug that can alter the function of the brain.”

They must’ve found me using my records from the pharma trials.

“Alter it to do what?”

The other man spoke. “Sir, we’ll be honest with you, we don’t really know how it works, but we do know what it does. They claim that it’ll give you access to all of your memories.”

“Uh, I pretty much have access to all of them already.”

“Oh, I’m sorry if you misunderstood me sir. I meant all of your memories. Past and future.”

“Wait, what?”

“I can understand your confusion. The way they explained it to us is that the brain knows everything about us. There is a portion of the brain that predicts what we’re going to do, our actions in the next couple of minutes or throughout the day. According to them, the function of that part of the brain is restricted. They’ve figured out a way to completely unlock that part of the brain, which will theoretically predict your movements and actions for the rest of your life.”

I just stare at him, speechless for a few seconds, not knowing how to react.

“Is this a joke?”

The other man looks slightly infuriated now. “Look, sir, are you willing to participate in this trial or not? We do have other places to go to.” I don’t really have any friends, at least not anyone who would go to these lengths for a practical joke.

“So, uh, what would I have to do?”

“If you say yes, we’d come back in the afternoon to pick you up and take you to the facility where the trial is being conducted. After you take the pill, the people over at R&D will keep you for some time to observe whether or not it worked. Then we’d let you go back to your regular life, but we would observe you over the next year to see the pill’s effects on your behaviour and health.”

“The next year ? Wait, wait, how much would I get paid for this?”

“Ten thousand dollars immediately after you take the pill. Forty thousand after that in installments over the next year.”

Holy shit. “Fifty thousand dollars!?”

“Yes, sir.”

I realised why they had chosen me. Someone who’s lonely, strapped for cash, but also someone who had enough intellect not to go crazy after taking the pill. Well, at least immediately after.

“So, sir, are you in or out? It’s completely up to you.”

It was a huge decision, too huge for me to make my mind up on the spot. But one simple fact allowed me to make a quick judgement. I hated this life. I’d do anything to escape it.

“Okay. I’ll do it.”

“Good. We’ll come to pick you up at 3 o’clock sharp. Thank you, sir.”

And with that, they both got up and left. A couple of minutes later, I realised I hadn’t asked them for any ID whatsoever, and that there was no way any part of what they said was possible. After half an hour, I’d dismissed the whole incident as an absurd prank. But sure enough, exactly at 3:00 PM, I heard my doorbell ring again.

Curious, I rapidly opened the door to find one of the men who had talked to me this morning standing in front of me.

“What happened to the other guy?”

“He didn’t think it was necessary for him to be here for this.”

I recalled that the man who hadn’t come this time was the more irritated one.

I was taken to a black SUV and notioned by the man to get in the back. As I sat down, he suddenly gripped my hand firmly and swiftly injected the contents of a syringe into my forearm.

Startled, I shouted, “Hey! What the hell is this!”

“Sorry sir, I can’t let you figure out the location of the facility we’re going to.”

Even as he finished his sentence, I started feeling drowsy. Within thirty seconds, I was completely knocked out.

I woke up to somebody shaking me lightly.

“Sir? Sir? Good, you’re up. Let’s go.”

He held my arm as I was groggily led through many hallways in an old, empty building. We eventually stopped in front of a door with a piece of paper stuck to it. On the sheet the words ‘CANDIDATE 14’ were printed.

“Good luck, sir.” He opened the door and notioned at me to go in.

And so I find myself here, with an impossible choice.

The woman in front of me is saying something.

“Hey, Ryan? Ryan?”

“Sorry, uh, Sara, I think I just zoned out for a minute there.”

“It’s okay. So, I think you’ve already been briefed about what we’re trying to do here. But we have to make sure you’re fully committed to this. If you want, you can say no, walk out of here, and pretend like none of this ever happened. If you say yes, there’s no going back, so choose carefully.”

“I assume that you’re-”

“One of the scientists involved in this project, yes. I’m here to answer any and all questions you might have.”

“Okay. So what happens if I walk out of here and try to contact the media about all this?”

She laughs a little and says perfectly calmly, “Rest assured we’ll ensure that doesn’t happen.”

I didn’t feel like inquiring further about the meaning of her ominous words.

“So I was thinking about the science behind it, the unlocking the potential of the brain thing, and I just-”

“Wait, wait, wait, that’s what they told you we’re doing? God, they never listen properly. That’s a very crude and shallow way of describing what we’re doing here. It’s like comparing, uh, knowing the way from here to your house to all of Google Maps. Anyway, just leave the science to me. Have you been notified about all of the effects of the pill?”

“They said I’ll see my future?”

“Well, yeah, that’s basically it in a nutshell. You will have all of your memories from your future.”

“But how is that possible? Isn’t the future uncertain?”

“No, it isn’t. Is your past uncertain? Certain events will take place in your life. The problem is just knowing what those events will be. We don’t fully understand the brain yet, and maybe one day we can figure out how it knows your future accurately. But for now, all we can tell you is that it’s guaranteed you’ll remember it all.”

“And it won’t melt my brain or anything?”

She laughs again, in the same slightly condescending manner as the last time, before replying, ”We’re way past that phase of testing. This is mostly just to see the psychological effects of the pill.”

“Has anyone ever taken the pill before?”

“People? No. Well, my coworkers are in the other rooms with other candidates, so maybe one of them has taken it by now. But you’re part of the first batch.”

“What do you think I should do? Should I take it?”, I ask, even though I know what she’s going to say.

“Look, I know you think I’m here to convince you to take it.”

Touché.

“But that’s counterintuitive to this project. If you’re not mentally ready for the pill’s effects and you take it, it’ll ruin our observations. If you’re sure you’re ready, then and only then should you take the pill.”

No one speaks for a minute.

“Philosophically speaking, this is a hot mess. There’s no objective way for me to decide whether or not to do this.”

“But an objective opinion doesn’t matter. You’re the one who’s here. You’re the one who’ll decide.”

“Well, even for me, there’s no easy solution. On first thought, I’ll be getting fifty thousand dollars, so of course I should say yes. Thinking a little more about it, I realise I’d be giving up my whole life to this, that I’d become a guinea pig for some experiment, so I should say no. But then again, at this point I don’t really have much of a life to give up in the first place.”

She takes a couple of seconds before she speaks.

“I take issue with your definition of giving up your life.”

“Why? I’d know what’s going to happen for the rest of my life, wouldn’t I?”

“Yes, but that doesn’t mean that you wouldn’t experience any of it. Just because you know what’s going to happen doesn’t mean that you won’t actually do it. When you rewatch a funny movie, you know you’ll still laugh at the funny parts, right?”

“Yeah, but-” “Even though you’ll know the pains and joys that the future holds for you, you’ll still experience them fully. The only difference is the foresight of what’s about to come. And honestly, most people would love having that foresight.”

“What do you mean?”

“Haven’t you ever had days where you didn’t take the time to appreciate when something good was happening for you? Where you were miserable for some unrelated reason and later regretted not being as happy as you could’ve been that day?”

“So you’re saying that because I’ll know when the joyous moments in my life would be, I could live those to the fullest.” “Exactly. And not only that, getting past the rough times would be easier too if you knew for certain that things would get better. The anxiety of your future during your bad times would vanish.”

“That’s a double-edged sword though.” “How so?”

“The lack of uncertainty would create a lack of excitement. I’d know exactly what’s in store for me for the years to come.”

“Again, that isn’t purely a negative thing. You lost everything at your job at Wall Street, right? Imagine how much better you would’ve handled that if you’d have known it was coming.”

“Maybe I would’ve handled it better, but all the joy I had during those years at my job would’ve been lost if I knew I was going to lose it eventually.”

“Are you saying that knowing something is temporary makes it harder to love?”

Wow. Unbeknownst to her, Sara had just beaten me with my own views about life and time.

“Okay, I admit you’ve been able to refute all my arguments.”

“I’m not arguing with you, Ryan. I believe in the work we’re doing here a hundred percent, and I wouldn’t be asking you to do this if I didn’t.”

“What exactly is the work that’s done here?”

“Well, we’re in R&D, so R&D. Research and development.”

“Experimental science, right?”

She nods.

“Then isn’t all of this just a way to try and control nature? To manipulate the things that come naturally to us, that make us fundamentally human?”

“Well, wait a second now-”

“Life isn’t meant to be lived with permanent certainty. We’re not supposed to live life to the fullest, we’re just supposed to live life. You said I wouldn’t have any regrets, right? But I’m supposed to. We learn from the mistakes we make. If I know everything about myself, I’ll have nothing else to achieve for the rest of my life.”

“Just because you have a knowledge of what will happen in your life doesn’t necessarily dictate that you don’t learn from it further. The full emotional consequences of the things you’re going to do will only manifest themselves after they actually happen. When you watch a football match that you’re sure your team will lose, you still feel bad after they lose even though you knew it would happen, right?”

“But that’s not entirely because I watch them lose. It’s also a matter of losing hope. Hope that maybe a miracle would happen, that something out of the ordinary occurs. If I do this, I would never have that hope anymore.”

I pause for a second.

“I just realised that taking this pill would mean I wouldn’t have any hope for the rest of my life. With all my actions defined, I can’t possibly believe anything will change.”

“Okay, this is going to sound harsh, but do you really have any hope right now? I’ve been through your file. You’re aggressively unemployed, you’re borrowing money left and right that we both know you’re never going to give back unless you do this, and you look like a shadow of the person you were 5 years ago. We’re giving you financial security, I think that’s hope enough.”

Even though none of what she said was new information to me, her blunt words broke me a little. I tried to hide my pain to preserve whatever dignity I had left.

“First of all, ouch. Secondly, I can scrape my way through. Who knows, I might win the lottery, or I could open a consultancy, or maybe even run a chain of successful yogurt shops. Who knows?”

“You could, if you take the pill.”

“That’ll ruin the feeling of achievement. It’ll feel like cheating, like it wasn’t me who figured out a path to success, but someone else who I’m now copying.”

“But won’t you feel that much more relieved knowing that your future is secure? If you think that you’ll lose all hope, you’ll also technically lose all fear.”

I consider this for a moment.

“But it comes back to the same thing. If I have no hope, if I have no fear, I don’t really have a reason to live.”

Sara’s finally beginning to look a little agitated now.

“I feel like I’m banging my head against a wall here. Knowledge and experience are two separate things, and one of them cannot make up for the absence of another. Just because you figured out how to tie a tie once by looking at a YouTube tutorial, doesn’t mean you know how to do it forever. And even if you learn all the steps to tying one, you’ll never really know until you can until you actually do it.”

“Wait, are you telling me you don’t know how to wear a tie?”

“Look, this discussion is endless. I can keep coming up with arguments and relevant metaphors and you can keep refuting them with your philosophy bullshit. I think it’s high time you decide. Are you in or out?”

I suddenly realise that this argument is really just another way for me to stall, to postpone the decision a bit longer. Enough. Now it’s time to take a call.

If I wasn’t as miserable and desperate as I am now, I wouldn’t have even considered taking the pill. But I hate my life. Fifty thousand dollars will do me a lot of good. I could clean myself up, shift somewhere better, maybe even meet someone. Even if I know what lies in store for me, so what? Whatever it is, it’ll definitely be better than the end point of my current trajectory. And maybe I’ll have a new sense of purpose. I’ll finally be part of something that’s not only about me.

“Okay. I’ll do it.”

I press the yes button in front of me. It flashes with green light, then a small door opens on the side of the button. I stick a couple of fingers inside and grab the one and only, almighty pill. It looks exactly like your run of the mill pharmacy shop medicine, a tiny red and white capsule containing god knows what inside it. I can’t really wrap my mind around the fact that all the stress and tension of today revolves around this one miniscule tablet.

“Great. Now all you’ve got to do now is swallow it”, Sara says. “In a minute or two, you’ll start to feel a little bit drowsy and then there’ll be a sudden prick in the back of your head. After that, you should be able to remember everything.”

I place the pill in my palm and slowly bring it up to my mouth. I can feel my hand getting clammy and my forehead starting to sweat. It feels more like a fifty-pound rock in my hand than a dainty little pill. As I get closer to swallowing it, I feel a sudden rush of adrenaline. Immediately, something clicks inside my head and just as I’m about to swallow it, I stop.

Sara looks confused.

“What’s the problem?”

I put my shaking hand down on the table and rest the pill there.

“I – I can’t do it.”

“Oh you’ve got to be kidding me”, she says as she looks at me, now visibly frustrated.

“I’m sorry”, I say as I walk out of the room, feeling a twinge of regret but also one of relief.

As I walk out the room, I see the man who had taken me here, standing guard next to the door.

“Sir? You chose not to take the pill in the end?”

“I couldn’t. Right as I was about to take it, my heart was beating, I was full of adrenaline, and I had no idea what was going to happen next. I can’t live my life without that feeling. Maybe someone else can. But not me.”

While the man led me out of the compound, I had time to think about what I had just done. I was anxious, regretful, unsure of myself and of my future- and I was loving every second of it.
