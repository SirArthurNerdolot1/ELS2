Camera: A Short Story

November 29, 2020

~Mudit Chand Narayan

The sound of the cold, steady stream of water was acting like a lullaby to her ears. It’s been twenty minutes of her sitting on the wooden bridge that somehow was the only passage from one side to another in the miles of the river’s flow.

She had her eyelids closed, and her ears were doing nothing but listening to the noises the water running underneath her was making as it was pummeling on the impenetrable rocks underlying the riverbed. Her legs were hanging into the river from the bridge-side. The occasional drizzles of icy-cold water were making her quiver as they touched her toes.

She was a photographer.

Had she looked up, the glorious mountains glistening in the north would have been her dream come true. She spent her childhood snaring the flowers of her garden in her camera, and her early twenties capturing her dreams; but today she was sitting stock-still.

She unconsciously broke what seemed an eternal stare, and palpated to her right where her camera was. Fumbling through the jacket and things lying there, her hand fell on the camera.

She groped it. Adjusting herself on the ridge of the wooden bridge, she delicately picked it up. With her eyes closed, she felt the button she once clicked to capture a million memories in that little frame.

She touched the lens, oh those lenses. How much she cared about them. The whole set she had, how she was proud of each of them. She knew their functions by heart, and she never shied off from bragging about her knowledge about cameras.

She rolled her fingers on the ridges of the lens, zooming in and out, and then left it in her lap.

“Riya!” She heard her name echoing the valley from a distance, which meant that it was her time to leave the tranquility of this valley, which was the only thing she enjoyed. She grasped on her shoes, and then her jacket.

But as she was picking up her jacket, she heard that particular “knock” too.

It was the lens. The other lens she had left on her jacket when he took her there on the bridge and left. It rolled, and rolled, and as she knew it was falling, she kept throwing her hands in different directions but could not get a hold of it.

It tossed to the edge and then it fell.

It took a last bump on her toe, and then it was gone. Gone in the roaring waves of the waterway rippling under her feet. An accident, an imperceptible recklessness, and she lost the lense she loved the most.

Just the way she lost her eyes three months ago in that accident.

The cold water had made her feet numb, more numb than her soul at the moment. And as the sound of waves crashing on lifeless stones went louder, a sob built up and went unnoticed.

The chilling wind was enough to freeze someone’s guts as she wore the jacket. “ Coming “, She replied to the voice that called her what seemed like a millennium ago and she clenched her camera to her chest.

A camera, which once was the other face of hope to her was not leaving any chance of reminding her of her frailing dreams and the feebleness of her knees. The lens that fell was continuously paining her with the fact that she couldn’t even see how it fell off the bridge like her fragile hopes tripped when she was hit by the car that day.

“ Click “, a painfully unnatural sound filled the valley. And though she could not see the pictures, she kept clicking as a tear rolled down her cheeks and disappeared midway.
