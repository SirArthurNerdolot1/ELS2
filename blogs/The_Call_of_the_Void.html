The Call of the Void

November 25, 2020

~Vijay Bharadwaj

Orange leaked from the clerestory. Ankush nudged me.

“What’s the time?”

“Almost six.”

“Man, wish we had some powers and stuff to get them out of this line, this hunger is uncontrollable now.”

“Yeah, I guess”

We’re not in some surreal dimension, Anks. Grow up. It was good food for the imagination, though, but we needed none of it right now. The queue at the Cultural Programme Registration Desk was dawdling. You couldn’t have seen the line moving if you weren’t in it for at least half-an-hour, like we were. The weight of the Les Paul on my shoulders was proving to be less tolerable minute by minute. Finally our turn came. We all smiled. The senior at the desk looked at us three.

“What’s the band called?”

“The Void”

Tushar came up with that name. He thought the moniker went well with our grungy, dark style of music. He was right – the name did feel apt.

“How long will your performance be?”

“5 minutes?”

“Yeah, 5 minutes.”

“Do you have a title for your performance that our anchors can use?”

Wait, we didn’t give it a thought. Suddenly, the two swagger looking dudes on either side looked a bit dazed. We started prodding each other and whispering – “Say something?!”

The desk interrupted the chaotic murmuring.

“Guys, if you haven’t decided a name for your performance yet, take some time and convey it to the anchor before your gig on the stage, fair?”

We yeah’d our way out of there after writing down our names and phone numbers on the list.

The sky had turned into a more serious orange. We were on our ways back home. Ankush started humming ‘Back It Up’ to the beats of our footsteps, which became faintly perceptible when Big T started patting his thighs to mimic the claps. It was the first song we’d written together as a power trio. Anks did the drums, Tushar did the bass and I did the axe (and we still do). I did most of the singing as well. We loved that song – it had banged at all the audiences we had performed it in front of and we were certainly going to start our performance this Friday with it.

“Hey you two, let’s meet tomorrow morning at 11. Practice ‘At Hell’s Door’. The chords are gonna need some serious preparation. We’ll do it again together when we meet.” We wrote ‘At Hell’s Door’ and ‘You Can Do It’ during the Summer Camp a couple of years back. We were going to perform these two as well in our 5-minute feat for the Cultural Programme of the School Fest ‘Angaara’. Writing songs like this would eventually become our refuge from the usual mundane. It was one essential dynamic that fueled this friendship.

I was near the Town Clinic when I saw this squirrel, bang in the middle of the road, absolutely still. It was probably enjoying getting baked in the sun or something – how do I know? I was looking at it the whole time – wondering what it’d do next. Suddenly, I see this grey Alto making a sharp turn from a decent distance. Any animal with a little aptitude would have easily shown a bit of reflex action and got out of the way. This cute little idiot however, decided to jump intentionally right into the bumper of the car when it came speeding closer. All I saw next was the car going past me. Why would it do that? I shoved away the thought quickly though, since it seemed equally idiotic to be thinking about each and every of those thousands of animals that become victim to such incidents.

“Rest in power, Mr. Acorns.”

Ways separated at the township ring road crossing and I reached home. Profuse sweating and a busy day at the rehearsal had dampened me literally and mentally, and I needed a towel, so I asked Sneha for it. A towel arrived flying from where the living room bends into the kitchen. While I was drying myself, something rustled under me. It was a letter, which had developed creases for obvious reasons. Next thing, my interest was piqued.

It was addressed to me. In the history of our living at this house, never ever had we received a letter addressed to me. We’d seen many improbable things – snakes in our bath, chasing monkeys inside our living room, Grandma threading a needle in the very first go, but this one was new. Maybe it was a conspiracy ploy by all the crores of Gods that exist in my faith, to keep me from creating a nuisance for Mom, Dad, Sneha or for that matter, anyone around me. They perceived me as this highly volatile and ambitious figure that can get upset or anxious or excited about ANYTHING. That’s what they say, at least. I respectfully disagree, however. Indian families do exaggerate things.

Coming back to the letter, it said on the cover:

“Mr. Vijay Bharadwaj,

F-21, Nand Gram,

Gandhi Road, Delhi-18”

and that wasn’t it. The handwriting. It almost looked typed. It was so perfect – it wouldn’t had given itself away if it wasn’t for the ink that got diffused at a few spots. Perfect Didot-style typography and ligatures. “Jeez, that’s some talent right there.”

Was it some famous artist that noticed our music and wanted to call us in to work with them? Nah, that would’ve been a stretch too far. We weren’t even the best band in our own school. Wait, I did turn all my work in before the deadline, didn’t I? Yeah I did. Besides, Miss Gautam wasn’t going to take the hardship of writing an actual letter, especially when I did attend her classes sincerely. Neither was it a handwriting that I recognized from before. My brain was on the verge of going bonkers.

But there were no stamps. No idea why. I hadn’t even opened the envelope yet, and it was raising an alarming number of question marks in my head. Sneha asked me to stop shrieking. I have always had suspicions about her not being right in the head.

So, I took out the sheet of paper inside the envelope and I remember gawking at it consistently for the next fifteen minutes. It said:

Welcome. Use it wisely.

Regards,

I thought the sender’s name would appear magically or something. I held it up against the lamp, tried shaking the paper, even put it in water, as the movies do it. Nothing appeared. And besides, welcome WHERE and use WHAT wisely?! This was probably a joke by one of my classmates. God bless their sense of humour, if they have any. There was no use for soaking wet junk like this. My aims for the dustbin have never been better.

I dozed off and woke up for dinner. At the dinner table, everything was pretty usual. The food was not the most exciting thing so I was engaged in thought about every other random thing that was in the room. Amongst the usual clatter of the cutlery, my fluttering eyes got distracted by a desperate Sneha trying to reach that last piece of pickle at the bottom of the jar. She kept poking a spoon at the bottom of the jar with increasing impetus and decreasing patience. As soon as my eyes focused at her, something brought a prompt change to her facial expression – as if some inexplicable power had captured the reins of her reason; it was a sudden change from a mildly displeased poker face to one of extreme frustration. Did some unknown force possess her? What possibly could’ve happened? The next thing that happens – there is a nasty shatter. Sneha threw the jar onto the floor in a fit of bitter vexation

and it got smashed into a thousand pieces, scattered all across the wooden tiling in the room. Fortunately, no shards of glass aimed itself towards the table, so there were no injuries.

“Are you mad or what? Why did you throw it?”

“Ugh, guess who has to go to the market again.”

“Sorry sorry sorry sorry, I don’t know how, I just… sorry.”

I remained silent. There was something wrong with how the course of things went today. The squirrel and then Sneha. It was not typical of what I would see every day. Was the letter also related to what happened today? The mental exhaustion caused by this mystery was too much to handle.

Not a good night’s sleep. I had already slept half of my evening with that siesta and all I was doing now was staring at the ceiling and thinking what on God’s lovely Earth was that all about.

There was certainly some third party involved. It couldn’t have just been a coincidence. Even if it was, my sense won’t accept its existence. And what was the letter about? Who wrote it? Why were there no stamps? What was the text about? Imagine a cartoon of a person and squiggle a big fat ‘Ugh’ on the head. That was me.

Was it really a possession of a power? Wait, did the power itself write the letter? That could’ve been a reason as to why there was no sender’s name. But what did it want from me? It seemed too surreal to be true.

But had there been incidents in other parts of the city, there must have been a report. Out of all us mortals, why did it only happen to me? For a good hour while I was thinking what did power would look like – nasty blood-lathered creature with horns or a ghost with a handlebar and a vintage monocle? Also, if I assume it only happened to me, do I happen to possess that power? I mean, things only happened when I looked at them. The squirrel was well and conscious and then it suddenly stops as soon as I see it? Sneha was on her own trying to get the pickle out and as soon as my eyes focused at her, she throws that jar away out of nowhere? People…do things…when I see them?

“Wish I could summon…”

A howling wind started blowing outside as soon as I thought of summoning the mysterious being. Eerie scenes. Suddenly, I was gasping for breath. I couldn’t see anything. Darkness, not even the faint moonlight that peeked through the curtains. Something held onto my throat tightly. I cried for help but even I couldn’t hear it. For a moment, there was peaceful oblivion and suddenly, it seemed like I was let go. There was light. My senses worked. I guess I knew now what the story was.

I had the power to enforce people’s intrusive thoughts upon looking at them. Inducing the Call Of The Void, as Google says. Some unnamed power had endowed this ability to me, out of nowhere and it expected me to use it wisely. Wait, didn’t I imagine having a power while I was in queue for the

registration? I had this power all that time? Well, shove it. I guess I am going to have some fun tomorrow.

I woke up in a jolly good mood. I felt like a superhero, with newborn abilities. My eyes were my weapons and my shields. And I felt pretty good about it. It was showtime for some much needed mayhem in this ordered world. The road joining my residence to the school was my arena. Starting from my house, the first victim was my pet Labrador Jimmy. My mom was watering the plants in the lawn right in front of Jimmy’s eyes. As soon as it got close to mom, I shifted my focus on Jimmy and it jumped straight into the wet soil under the watering can, to have a shower. The poor boy must have resisted the urge so badly! Jimmy got dirty in the cool mud and certainly enjoyed it, unlike my mom who was looking at her squished marigolds that she had grown with weeks of patience and care, with immense disappointment.

“Stupid creature.”

On my way, there were a lot of wild roadside flowers. They didn’t budge a bit when I directed my sight at them, so I guess plants don’t have intrusive thoughts. It would have been cool if they did.

The very next minute, a boy in his early teens came cycling from the turn ahead of me. It was time to put my powers to test again. A car followed from behind speeding past me. As soon as the car came parallel to the cycle, the powers captured control of the boy and induced the boy’s urge to speed his cycle into the car. Good thing, I looked away immediately, so all the boy experienced was a little wobble.

Phew, that was a close call. With great power, indeed comes great responsibility. Not too sure if I would care about it that much, though.

The road was didn’t have much bustle, so I couldn’t have any more lab rats to test on. I was assured, however, that I would get a lot of them when I reach school.

It was preparation time for everyone at school for the Programme so not a lot of studying was happening. I saw a corridor on the second floor with a lot of juniors involved in decorating the artboards. I walked through the pathway giving a generous amount of eye to every being. The peaceful passage transformed into a warzone of utter chaos when I trodded upon the scene. There were glue bottles and paintbrushes being hurled down the second floor. I am pretty sure a few would have jumped down, had I not closed my eyes immediately. One little chap tried to hang himself to a nail in the wall with both his hands like a house calendar and fell down, becoming a subject of immense laughter for those around him. One could film this in slow-motion and create a serious hero entry cutscene, no kidding. The intensity of things happening was incomprehensible. The most enjoyable part of all this was how quickly the victims of this power’s summons change their mind about doing what they had the urge to do, when I take my eyes off of them. It was instantaneous and highly comical to look at.

I went to an empty classroom where I was supposed to meet Ankush and Tushar for the rehearsal. “Hey, did you think of any names for the performance?”

“Oops.”

“Ugh, think of it and tell me soon. The anchors are pestering me for it, saying they need it for practicing their script.”

*my phone rings*

“If you sneak in a phone like that into school, remember to put it on silent at least.”, Anks said.

It was a call from Kshitij, my best friend from my old school. Man, 5 years since I last talked to him. Unfortunately the call got disconnected and a voicemail followed, which said,

“Hey Vijay, this is Kshitij from Bal Bharati. See, I am here with a few other of your classmates from your time and we wished if you could meet us here. Come to the tank terrace of the Acad Wing.”

The offer to meet Kshitij and a few of my other old schoolmates couldn’t be turned down. It was a lot of good memories with them, and I couldn’t wait to meet them after such a long time!

It was a 15 minute walk by road but one could reach there through an unpaved road through the township jungle in 10 minutes. So I took the shorter route. It was a dense jungle in those times, and had grown even more so, over these years. After 10 odd minutes of solitary trodding amongst these vines and shrubs and under these mighty canopies, a ray of light peeked the sea of green and there was Bal Bharati in front of me.

It was a desolate sight. It was probably a school holiday – no one to be seen around, not even the guards. The school hadn’t changed one bit in these years. Still the dusty brown paint on the patterned walls, the same rusty gates, the same corridors and even the classroom windows weren’t changed. I paced up the stairs to reach the rooftop where I meet Kshitij, Arhum, Kshuanish and Prashant.

“The five Pandavas are back, what say, my fellas!”

“Man, it’s been so long…”

A string of high-fives followed. It was an anecdotal trip down the memory lane. Everyone had their fair share of stories and incidents to converse about. Arhum even brought the classic salted wafers from the halwai near his residence, as he always used to do when such rooftop powwows were scheduled (no wonder he’s gained a sizable amount of weight in these years).

It was 3 pm. As we were talking to each other, I walked towards the small railing of the terrace to check the view out. The vines that grew, as if they eavesdropped on the melting pot of memories that was being filled here, the whole township that looked very small in scale. Even the famous Wall Of Confession of our terrace where a lot of cheesy rumours about student couples were born, was even more coloured by now. Guess the culture never died.

But as soon as I looked down, a strange power looked to dominate me. It was a very evident déjà vu, from last night. My senses withdrew from my body, there was darkness. My body felt heavier and out of my control. An uncalled-for power hindered my breathing. My shoulder drooped down like a withered Mimosa flower. Something was hammering my insides to make me jump down the railing. It seemed

like a coma. My consciousness got sapped out of my soul slowly. As hard as I tried to fight it by jerking my hands, it was pulling me to the edge as if I had no energy of my own, quite effortlessly. Then, there was nothing.

Next thing I know, Kshitij and others were around me, trying to wake me up. As I regained my senses, I looked around at the blurry world around me. And then,

*Missed Call from The Void*

Call of… the Void?

*1 new message from The Void*

“Back At You.”

Back at me? My body started to shiver at the sight of this message. I felt helpless and limp. Blots of darkness again started appearing in the nooks of my eye. What was happening and what did I do to deserve it? I was breathing heavily, as if a choked throat was let go. I coughed and even threw up. Prashant passed some water for me to drink, but then…

*2 new messages*

“The first words of the three songs that we’re going to perform!”

“The name for our performance!”

The sky was orange. Yet again.
